# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Founders, operators, and compliance managers at startups, SMEs, and enterprises across Africa and global markets, navigating data protection, regulatory compliance, intellectual property, and corporate governance. They typically arrive comparing advisory options and need to assess credibility quickly — the primary job on this site is evaluating whether Markly can be trusted with legal/compliance work, then booking a consultation.

## Product Purpose

Markly Africa's marketing site for an AI-assisted legal, compliance, and IP advisory practice. The site exists to build enough trust — through clarity, precision, and visible human oversight of AI-driven work — that a visitor converts to a booked consultation. Success looks like: a visitor understands what Markly does within one scroll, believes the "AI drafts, human reviews" claim because they can see it demonstrated (not just asserted), and takes the primary CTA.

## Brand Personality

Bold, professional, intelligent, reliable, modern, human-centered. Core thesis, carried through every page: **"AI-powered capability with human professional expertise"** — AI-driven tools accelerate research and monitoring, but every recommendation is reviewed by a qualified human expert before it reaches the client. This is the single biggest differentiator in the actual product and should be shown, not just claimed.

## Anti-references

- **Generic AI-startup SaaS template.** Gradient text, hero-metric templates (big number + small label + supporting stats), identical icon-in-a-circle card grids repeated section after section, a tiny uppercase tracked eyebrow above every heading. This is the default a bare LLM prompt produces for "advisory/compliance startup" and is explicitly what Markly should not read as.
- **Stodgy traditional law-firm site.** Navy-and-gold palettes, stock photography of handshakes and skylines, dense unbroken text walls, no visual personality. Markly is modern and technology-enabled, not a 1990s law-firm brochure.
- **Overly playful or consumer-startup tone.** No gimmicks, mascots, or casual/meme-adjacent copy — the brand needs to read as authoritative for a compliance/legal audience even while feeling contemporary.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the human review, don't just claim it.** The brand's core differentiator ("AI drafts, human reviews") should be demonstrated visually wherever it's plausible — e.g. the redline motif (an AI-drafted line struck through next to the human-corrected version) — rather than stated once in a paragraph and forgotten.
2. **Ground the visual vocabulary in the subject's own artifacts.** Reach for legal/compliance vernacular — filings, exhibits, schedules, seals, declarations, registration marks — instead of generic tech/SaaS iconography (rockets, gears, abstract blobs) or numbered 01/02/03 scaffolding.
3. **Vary structure across sections; no repeated template.** A single "eyebrow + heading + card grid" pattern reused section after section reads as generic regardless of correct brand colors. Each section should earn its own layout from what it's actually communicating.
4. **Practice what you preach.** The brand promises precision and rigor: the execution itself (contrast ratios, spacing consistency, responsive behavior, motion that respects reduced-motion preference) has to be flawless, because sloppy craft directly undermines the "reliable, intelligent" trust claim.
5. **Restraint over decoration.** Orange is a ~10% accent color, never the dominant surface and never plain text on a light background. Purple carries brand identity without becoming an overused background. The page should feel authoritative and edited, not maximalist.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA is the floor (per SKILL.md's Contrast Requirements table); push to AAA wherever the existing palette allows it for free — several core pairings already clear AAA (purple-on-off-white 14.2:1, white-on-purple 15.0:1) and should stay there rather than being diluted. Orange (`#FE5F1B`) is never used as text on light backgrounds (fails AA at ~2.9:1); it's approved only as background/accent, on purple (4.90:1), or as large/semibold white-on-orange UI text (3.06:1). Reduced motion is respected site-wide — every entrance/reveal animation (fade-in-up, seal-in, redline strike, scroll-reveal) has a `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` override that removes motion without losing content.
